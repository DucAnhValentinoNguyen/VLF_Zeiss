Endoscopy Calibration Study

Does in-domain self-supervised pretraining on unlabeled endoscopy video frames improve how honest a vision-foundation model's confidence is — not just how accurate it is — when evaluated zero-shot? Four pretraining settings, measured before and after, plus a case study in catching a training run that looked fine and was not.

Scope note: these are frozen vision encoders evaluated by nearest-neighbour retrieval — there is no language model or text head in this study.

Before vs after self-supervised pretraining

Four settings — {LeJEPA, DINO v1} × {SigLIP‑2 ViT‑B/16 init, ImageNet ViT‑B/16 init} — each measured zero-shot on HyperKvasir (five endoscopy tasks: 20‑way finding, 4‑way category, upper‑vs‑lower GI tract, pathological‑vs‑other, and zero-shot polyp segmentation), then re-measured after a short in-domain self-supervised adaptation on GastroNet‑5M, an unlabeled endoscopy-image corpus. “Zero-shot” means a weighted k-nearest-neighbour vote on frozen features (k = 20, τ = 0.07) — no classifier is ever trained; the backbone is asked to carry the whole task on its own.

What the baseline says

  • ImageNet init beats SigLIP-2 on every task, on both discrimination (accuracy, NLL) and mostly on calibration — the opposite of the pre-registered sanity guess, that the web-scale contrastive model would transfer better. For endoscopy k-NN it does not: supervised ImageNet features are more linearly separable here, and their patch tokens carry far more local structure (segmentation Dice 0.67 vs 0.31).
  • hkv_tract is flagged — confirmed structural, not leakage. A dedicated ontology audit found 23 of 23 HyperKvasir findings are tract-exclusive (each occurs in upper-GI or lower-GI images only, never both), so upper-vs-lower-GI is recoverable from coarse image content almost by construction — independent of the accuracy number itself. Treated as an interpretation caveat, recorded alongside every result.
  • Temperature scaling is not uniformly helpful. For ImageNet, T > 1 and post-T ECE sometimes rises (findings: .048 → .111): the k-NN “logits” are not true logits, and a single scalar fit on a 15% cal split can over-correct. For SigLIP-2 on category/pathology, T < 1 and post-T ECE improves. The pre-vs-post-adaptation delta is the intended read, not the absolute value.
  • The 20-way findings task is imbalanced. Balanced accuracy ~0.61 (imagenet) / ~0.50 (siglip2) against raw accuracy ~0.85 — strong on common findings, weak on rare ones, AUROC still ~0.95.

Flag — LeJEPA on the ImageNet init is a genuine, severe failure mode

Query accuracy on hkv_findings collapsed from 82.4% before to 19.9% after adaptation (AUROC 0.95 → 0.67); every classification task lost 19–62 accuracy points. This was checked against a pipeline bug and ruled out: the pre-adaptation baselines in the same evaluation sweep are normal, the other three settings in the same sweep are normal, and the adaptation run itself shows no instability — loss fell smoothly, no non-finite-loss skips, effective rank stayed at 33.9 (healthy; classic representation collapse reads ~1). AUROC staying above 0.5 confirms the features still carry some signal — the k-NN vote is just badly miscalibrated to the point of being nearly useless at the decision threshold.

Reading: LeJEPA's isotropy-regularising term aggressively reshapes the batch toward a standard Gaussian. Applied full-backbone to an already highly class-structured supervised ImageNet embedding, it appears to overwrite that structure faster than the light layer-wise learning-rate decay can protect it — the representation stays spread out (rank) but loses its linear/k-NN separability. A gentler-optimisation follow-up (lower base LR, stronger layer-wise decay) is the natural next experiment.

The story across all four settings

  • DINO is the reliable adapter. It improved calibration and/or segmentation from both initialisations, with no accuracy cost anywhere. From SigLIP-2 the gains are large (ECE −0.01 to −0.06, seg Dice +0.40); from the already-strong ImageNet init they are smaller but still net positive.
  • LeJEPA is powerful but initialisation-sensitive. From SigLIP-2 (room to improve) it trades accuracy for markedly better calibration and a strong segmentation gain (+0.13 Dice). From ImageNet (already excellent) it is destructive — the aggressive regulariser has nothing to gain and a great deal to lose.
  • Findings and segmentation are where adaptation matters most for the three well-behaved settings; the near-saturated tract/pathology tasks barely move either way.
  • Practical takeaway: if the deployment init is a strong supervised backbone, prefer a gentle self-distillation objective (DINO) for in-domain adaptation; reserve an aggressive distributional regulariser (LeJEPA) for weaker starting points, or pair it with a much smaller learning rate when starting from a strong supervised init.

CASE STUDY
Reliability engineering

The LR = 0 bug

The first full-scale training job started on an A100 the morning of 2026-09-08. Watching its scalar dashboard caught something the smoke tests could not: it was not training at all.

Symptom

Learning rate flat at 0.000e+00 for over 10,000 steps. Loss flat at 0.737 over the same span. The variance-hinge loss term pinned at its floor. Zero checkpoints written to the run directory.

Two independent bugs compounded:

  1. The framework's own step counter never advanced. The training step called the raw optimizer's .step() instead of the wrapped one — a workaround for an intermittent CPU hang on a login node, kept in place on GPU where it was never needed. On GPU that bypass means the framework's step counter stays at 0. Consequences: the cosine learning-rate schedule, keyed on that counter, sits at its warmup-step-zero value — LR ≡ 0, so every optimizer step is a no-op; and the checkpoint trigger, keyed on the same counter, never fires either — a crash-and-resume would have restarted from scratch, forever.
  2. The data pipeline's notion of “one epoch” was 12× too long. An epoch-length setting meant for the whole pipeline had been applied per data-loading worker instead, so with 12 parallel workers one logical epoch became 12× longer than intended (roughly 5 hours). The other checkpoint trigger, keyed on epoch-end, would not have fired inside the job's time limit either.

Together: the run could produce nothing usable, and would have looped indefinitely, silently, across every restart.

Fix

1. Use the wrapped optimizer step on GPU (the raw bypass kept only for CPU smoke tests) — the step counter advances, so the schedule and the checkpoint trigger both work.

2. Move the epoch-length setting to apply after the parallel workers are merged back into one stream, not before — verified directly: request N batches, get exactly N, stable across epochs and worker counts.

The GPU smoke test had passed because a handful of steps at a tiny batch size sits entirely inside learning-rate warmup — the rate is near-zero there whether or not the counter moves, and the smoke test only checks that a checkpoint file appears and that resuming from it works, which the epoch-end trigger did handle at that scale. The bug only showed up under a full-scale run, which is exactly why the run was being watched live rather than fired and checked on the next weekday.
